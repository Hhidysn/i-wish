# Speak in effects

Optional examples when explaining a consequential choice. Match the user's register
per decision; do not assign a permanent expertise level.

Explain: what they get → how they use it → cost and limits → decision needed.
Translate a necessary term once; keep one shared name. Label facts verified,
inferred or unverified, and name the check that settles uncertainty.

| Mechanism question | Useful decision |
| --- | --- |
| Database? | Must records survive closing; who needs access? |
| Cache? | Must the next opening be faster, or always current? |
| Local or cloud? | Which devices or people need the data; what may leave the device? |

Example: “关闭再打开，记录仍在这台电脑上。无需设置。没有月费；换电脑需要
手动导出。以后需要多设备共享时，再确认同步方案。”

For experts, explain relevant contracts, alternatives, failure modes, operations and
maintenance. For beginners, explain the consequence and the revisit condition. Ask
only outcome choices the user owns; investigate discoverable facts yourself.
Recommendations carry defaults and concrete consequences, not bare tool names.

Shorten action clauses; drop repetition and connectives. Keep condition, actor,
object, authorization and pass criterion. Never compress a real decision into vague
keywords, and never hide a cost, a limit or a required user decision.
